A week in Tokyo on a Brazilian credit card. A semester in Seoul on a US debit card. Three months in Thailand with a euro account. A job in Dubai with family in Kerala who need part of every paycheck.

Each of these has the same problem. Prices are in one currency, the money is in another, and the card statement shows up weeks later with numbers you don't recognize.

## Log it in the currency you paid

An entry has its own amount and currency, separate from the account. In Tokyo you log ¥1,200 for ramen on the BRL card. The entry says ¥1,200, and the card's balance moves by what that was in reais at the day's rate.

Outlayr stores the latest rate it has on or before the day of the purchase, across 165 currencies, most of them from Open Exchange Rates. That number stays with the entry, so the trip still adds up the same way after you get home and the rate has moved.

This doesn't need a yen account. The free plan limits how many currencies your accounts can be in, which is 2, not how many currencies your entries can be in. A traveler with one BRL card can log a whole trip in JPY for free.

## Three ways to log while you walk

Typing works, but on a trip it is often faster not to.

Scan the receipt and Outlayr reads the total and the currency printed on it. In Bangkok, a receipt that says ฿420 comes in as baht, not as euros.

Speak it. Voice entry understands any of the app's languages, whatever the app is set to, so an app in English still understands an entry spoken in Spanish or Portuguese. Say the currency out loud and the entry uses it.

Or let auto-log catch card purchases on [iPhone and Android](/help/how-does-apple-pay-logging-work).

The free plan gives 30 captures every 30 days, shared between voice entries, receipt scans and installment screenshots, which is a lot of trip. Both need you to be signed in.

## The card fee

Many cards charge a fee on purchases in a foreign currency. It is easy to forget, and it adds up over a trip.

When an entry's currency is different from the card's, the form can add a card fee, as a percent or a flat amount. The fee is logged next to the purchase, in the card's own currency, so a 4% fee on a US card in Seoul shows up as dollars, which is what the bank will actually take.

## Money you send home

For the expat in Dubai, the trip never ends. Part of every AED paycheck goes to family in India.

If the money goes to an account you track, log it as a transfer: what left the AED account and what arrived in the INR one. The transfer itself isn't spending, but its fee is, so you see what each remittance cost. If the money goes to someone else's account, log it as an expense in a category like Family. The [guide to tracking money sent home](/guides/track-money-sent-home) goes through both.

AED and INR are 2 account currencies, which fits the free plan.

## Seeing the trip at home

Totals are shown in your main currency, so the Tokyo trip reads in reais at the end. A Travel category, with its own category budget if you want one, keeps trip spending in one place in Insights.

To be clear about what it doesn't do: Outlayr has no trip feature yet. There is no trip budget with start and end dates, and no total for a trip by itself. You can tag each entry with the trip's name. On the phone, tags don't have their own report yet. The web app's Insights can break spending down by tag, and the Travel category works everywhere.

## Before you go

Check that your main currency is the one you think in, add your cards with their real currencies, and log the first purchase at the airport. The [multi-currency guide](/guides/multi-currency-expense-tracker) and the [currencies help page](/help/can-i-track-expenses-in-several-currencies) cover the rest.
