A lot of people earn in one currency and live in another. A designer in Lagos invoices a client in the US and gets paid in USD. A translator in Lisbon works for a UK agency, gets paid in GBP and pays rent in EUR. A virtual assistant in Manila is paid in USD and buys groceries in PHP. A developer in Bogotá signs contracts in USD and spends in COP.

It is the same money, but it lives in two places, and the rate between them moves every day. Most trackers ask you to pick one currency and convert by hand. Outlayr keeps both.

## One account per currency

Start with an account for each currency you actually hold. For the designer in Lagos, that is a USD account where the client's payments land and an NGN account for daily spending.

The free plan holds two account currencies, which covers being paid in one currency and living in another. A third, say EUR savings next to USD and NGN, is part of [Pro](/pricing).

Each account shows its balance in its own currency. The USD account says how many dollars are left, not a converted guess.

## Each entry keeps its own currency

An entry has its own amount and currency, separate from the account it came out of. If the translator in Lisbon pays a GBP software bill with a EUR card, the entry says £12, and the card's balance moves by what that cost in euros.

When you save an entry, Outlayr stores the latest exchange rate it has on or before the day of the purchase. Change the amount, currency or date later and it looks the rate up again. Otherwise that number stays put, so a report from March still shows what March cost, even after the rate moved.

Outlayr supports 165 currencies. Most rates come from Open Exchange Rates, checked every hour.

## Moving money between them

The moment that usually gets lost is the conversion. You move $500 from the USD account to the PHP account, and fewer pesos arrive than the day's rate promised.

In Outlayr that is a transfer. Each side has its own amount: what left the USD account and what arrived in the PHP one. The rate is worked out from those two numbers, or you can type the rate you got. If the platform charged a fee, it goes on the side that paid it, in that account's currency.

After a few months, the gap between what you were paid and what reached your spending account is right there in the transfers, not lost inside one blended balance.

## Income that repeats, and income that doesn't

A retainer that arrives on the same day every month can be a repeating income entry. When it comes due, Outlayr adds it with its scheduled date the next time you open the app, so the USD account goes up without you typing it.

Project payments that land whenever a client pays are better logged as they arrive. A repeating entry for money that might not come makes the month look better than it is.

If you get paid on a fixed day, you can also move the start of the month. In Settings, the month can start on any day from 1 to 28. Insights and new monthly budgets then run from that day, so the month starts when the money arrives, not on the calendar's 1st.

## Seeing the whole month in one currency

Accounts stay in their own currencies, but you also pick one currency the app adds everything up in. The developer in Bogotá might pick COP, because that is what the month is spent in. Someone saving toward a USD goal might pick USD.

Totals in that currency start from the rate stored with each entry, so switching the display currency doesn't rewrite what past purchases cost.

## What it doesn't do

Outlayr doesn't do invoices or taxes. It tracks what came in and what went out, in the currencies it actually moved in. You log a client payment the way you log a coffee: type it, say it, or let auto-log on [iPhone and Android](/help/how-does-apple-pay-logging-work) catch the purchases.

If you also send money home to family, [this guide](/guides/track-money-sent-home) covers that side. The [currencies help page](/help/can-i-track-expenses-in-several-currencies) has the short version of everything above.
