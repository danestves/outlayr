Households that try to budget together often end up with one person doing the logging. A couple in Toronto keeps a spreadsheet only one of them opens. A family in São Paulo has three people buying groceries and one phone that knows about it. Housemates in London split the electricity bill in a group chat and lose track by March.

The problem is rarely the budget. It is that the entries live in one person's phone.

## A profile is its own ledger

In Outlayr, a profile is a separate ledger: its own accounts, budgets, entries and main currency. Everyone starts with one. With Pro you can add others, for example "Family" next to "Personal", so household money and your own don't mix.

A shared profile is one of those, with other people in it. A couple in Nairobi can keep a "Home" profile in KES for rent, food and school fees, while each of them keeps a personal profile of their own.

## Inviting someone

Open the profile and share it. Outlayr makes a link, `tryoutlayr.com/join/` followed by a code, that you send however you like. It works for 7 days. Open the profile's members again while it is open and the same code gets a fresh 7 days. Once it runs out, you get a new link.

The person opens the link, signs in, and taps Join. The profile appears next to their own, and they switch between them at the top of the Log.

Up to 5 people can join a profile, on top of the owner. That covers a couple, a family with teenagers, or a flat of housemates.

## What everyone sees

Everyone in the profile sees the same accounts, budgets and entries on their own phone, and can add and edit entries. Entries from the other current members show who logged them, so "who bought this" stops being a question.

Budgets are shared too. If the family in São Paulo sets R$2,000 a month for groceries, every purchase from every phone counts against the same number, and everyone sees the same "a day for the days left" figure. If the month is running hot, everyone sees it while there is still time to change it.

Your personal profile stays yours. Nobody in the shared profile sees it.

## Who needs Pro

The owner of the profile needs [Pro](/pricing) to invite people and to keep the profile syncing. The people who join don't need Pro of their own. One subscription covers the household.

A shared profile syncs through Outlayr's servers, because that is how two phones see the same ledger. So everyone in it signs in.

If the owner's Pro lapses, members can't sync the shared profile until it is back.

## When someone leaves

Anyone who joined can leave a shared profile. The profile leaves their devices, and the entries they logged stay for everyone else, so leaving doesn't punch holes in last month.

The owner can also remove a member. They lose access on their devices, and their entries stay. If their app is open when it happens, the profile goes away on its own.

## Owing each other

Shared profiles are for money a household spends together. For money people owe each other, Outlayr has people: an account per person whose balance says who owes whom. The housemates in London can keep one for each flatmate, so the £45 one of them covered for the internet bill doesn't get forgotten.

To be clear about what it doesn't do: Outlayr doesn't split a bill between people for you, and it doesn't settle up. If you want a quick split, the [split expenses calculator](/tools/split-expenses-calculator) does the math.

## Picking a setup

Some couples put everything in one pot. Some split shared bills by income. Some keep "yours, mine and ours". A shared profile fits any of them, because it only holds what you decide to put in it. The [guide to budgeting as a couple](/guides/budget-app-for-couples) walks through the three setups.
